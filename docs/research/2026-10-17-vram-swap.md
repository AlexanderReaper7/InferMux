- Date: 2026-10-17
- Status: research for the user to decide on; nothing here is decided or built
- Asked by the user at 14:13 CEST ("i need a solution to the vram problems. something like swap for vram") and narrowed at 14:16 CEST ("when running a game, though the game does fit in vram, it collides with other stuff")
- Measured by the agent on strix, RTX 3080 10 GB, driver 615.78.08, between 14:20 and 14:44 CEST. The scripts are in [2026-10-17-vram-swap/](2026-10-17-vram-swap/); how each number was taken is in [Verification](#verification)

## Answers first

1. **There is no swap for graphics memory on Linux.** The NVIDIA driver never pages one process's Vulkan, OpenGL or `cudaMalloc` memory out to make room for another's. A new allocation past the free memory fails, with `VK_ERROR_OUT_OF_DEVICE_MEMORY` for Vulkan and `CUDA_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY` for CUDA, both measured here. NVIDIA says so for Linux in [Vulkan dos and don'ts](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/vulkan-dos-donts/): "There is no OS memory manager on Linux that mitigates over-commitment by automatically performing paging operations on memory objects." The one thing that does page is CUDA managed memory (unified memory), and only the managed process's own pages move.
2. **The game's collision today was a model loaded mid-game, not the desktop.** `infermux-hold` cleared the card at Warframe's start, and Warframe read a 5,575 MiB budget. Eleven minutes later, at 13:30:23 CEST, dikt's English key warmed `qwen3-asr-1.7b` onto the card. 24 s after that Warframe logged `Only 1GB / 10GB video memory free` and ran the remaining 46 minutes with a texture budget of 0.64-1.25 GB, down from 2.0-2.9 GB. The model stayed loaded until a pause by hand at 14:18:40, after the game had exited.
3. **The desktop holds about 3.9 GB that `infermux-hold` leaves alone**, of which a game mode can free about 0.5 GB cheaply (wallpaper 330 MB, ComfyUI's empty CUDA context 216 MB). The two largest parts cannot be freed while the session runs: the compositor at 0.4-1.1 GB, and 1.18 GB that nvidia-smi attributes to no process.
4. **Unified memory makes the game's allocations succeed, but does not fix what the game reads at start.** A Vulkan program beside an embedder in unified memory was told its budget was 756 MB, then allocated 3,840 MB. A game that sizes itself from the budget, as Warframe does, still starts small. Meanwhile the evicted model ran 40 times slower (Octen) or 55 times slower (Qwen3.5-4B).
5. **Only Warframe launches through `infermux-hold`.** ARC Raiders and Call of Duty: Ghosts start without it.

The agent's recommendation is in [Recommendation](#recommendation), and the choices that are the user's in [Decisions for the user](#decisions-for-the-user).

### What the driver does when the card is over-committed

The card runs the closed kernel module 615.78.08 (`open = false` in nixcfg's `modules/nixos/nvidia.nix`), with Addressing Mode `None` (no HMM), PCIe Gen4 x16, BAR1 16 GB, 315 MiB reserved and `uvm_global_oversubscription=1`. All of it read from `nvidia-smi -q`, `/proc/driver/nvidia/version` and `/sys/module/nvidia_uvm/parameters`.

The tests used two stand-ins. `vkhog` takes device-local Vulkan memory as a game would, then fills all of it once a second and prints the fill rate and the heap budget from `VK_EXT_memory_budget`. `cudahold.py` takes the same memory through CUDA, plain (`cuMemAlloc`, what llama.cpp does by default) or managed (`cuMemAllocManaged`, what llama.cpp does under `GGML_CUDA_ENABLE_UNIFIED_MEMORY=1`). It then prefetches the allocation back onto the card each second and prints how long that took.

| test | first holder | then | what happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| T7 | Vulkan 4,864 MB | plain CUDA asks 3,000 MB with 1,044 free | `cuMemAlloc_v2: 2 out of memory`. Nothing was paged |
| T8 | Vulkan 2,048 MB | a second Vulkan program asks for more than is free | it got 4,096 MB, then allocation 16 failed with `VkResult -2` (`VK_ERROR_OUT_OF_DEVICE_MEMORY`). The first kept its memory and its fill rate |
| T4a | managed CUDA 3,000 MB | Vulkan asks 5,888 MB; its budget at start said 2,925 MB | Vulkan got all 5,888 MB in 1.23 s by evicting the managed pages. The CUDA prefetch took 248-834 ms (3.5-11.8 GB/s) while Vulkan held them, and 2 ms again after Vulkan freed them |
| T6 | Vulkan 4,864 MB | managed CUDA 3,000 MB with 941 free | the managed side got about 930 MB on the card and moved the rest in and out at 7.3 GB/s (400 ms per pass), every second. The Vulkan fill rate stayed at 421-578 GB/s, except one reading of 112 GB/s while the CUDA side allocated |
| swapbench 4 | Octen under unified memory, loaded | Vulkan asks free + 3,000 MB; budget at start said 756 MB | Vulkan got 3,840 MB in 1.39 s (0.03-0.05 s when nothing needs evicting). See [Part 2](#unified-memory) for what Octen did |

So a managed allocation gives way to anyone's allocation, and nothing else gives way. The budget a Vulkan program reads is its own usage plus the free memory, and managed pages that it could evict still count as used.

`VK_EXT_pageable_device_local_memory`, which vkd3d-proton enables, sets priorities among one process's own allocations. It does not let one process take another's memory, which matches NVIDIA's text above. When either of two `vkhog` processes enabled it, the second one failed `vkCreateDevice` (`-1` or `-3`); with neither, both ran. Games under vkd3d-proton start beside cosmic-comp and Firefox every day, so this is likely an artifact of the test and not a driver limit. It is unexplained.

A fill rate that drops by up to 3 times is no evidence of paging. T7b showed the same drop, from 533 to 174 GB/s, with nothing else on the card. The card had clocked down (P3, 450 MHz).

## Part 2: model swapping

This part answers the user's first question, about Octen and the reranker swapping on every search, 503 `gpu_no_room` while the zbox is busy, and pairs that do not fit (0029).

### Loads are already fast from the page cache

Octen-Embedding-4B Q8_0 (4.28 GB file), with InferMux's flags, started by hand on port 5291, from start to `/health` 200:

| case | time |
|---|---|
| file evicted from the page cache (`posix_fadvise DONTNEED`, `fincore` 0 B) | 3.08 s |
| file in the page cache, three runs | 1.01-1.05 s |
| through InferMux in production, 14:29:10 CEST, start to first embedding | 1.35 s |
| Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M, page cache | 1.12-1.73 s |
| jina-reranker-v3.5 (nixcfg's measurement, 2026-10-06) | 2.2 s |

With 96 GB of RAM every model file stays in the page cache, so a swap already copies from RAM and costs about one second per model. Pinned host copies, or a park-and-restore like vLLM's sleep mode, could only take that second down to the PCIe copy time, about 0.2-0.3 s for 4 GB at 12-25 GB/s. That is an estimate; nothing here measured it. The 6-8 s per search the user saw is two loads in a row plus waiting behind each other, not slow loads.

llama.cpp 0.6.0 has `--sleep-idle-seconds`, which frees the model while idle and keeps the process. Its one try here failed with `CUDA error: out of memory` at warm-up, with 6,110 MB free against Octen's 5,888 MiB. The retry never ran. The user restarted strix at 14:45:06 CEST, and the rule of 2026-10-17 that only InferMux may host a model now forbids the agent's own `llama-server`. It is not measured.

### Unified memory

`GGML_CUDA_ENABLE_UNIFIED_MEMORY=1` makes llama.cpp allocate with `cudaMallocManaged`.

| case | Octen single query, p50 / max | Octen 8 chunks of about 1,000 tokens | Qwen3.5-4B prompt / generation |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain, resident | 16 / 36 ms | 1.71-1.80 s after a first round of 3.01 s | 3,247-3,670 / 122-139 t/s |
| unified, card otherwise free | 15-16 / 25-31 ms | 1.61-1.72 s after a first round of 2.69-2.75 s | not measured |
| unified, Vulkan stand-in took 3,840 MB from it | 632 / 1,561 ms | 8.45 s | |
| unified, stand-in holds all but 1,500 MB | | | 402 / 2.4 t/s, 54.8 s for 128 tokens |
| unified, after the stand-in left | first query 264 ms, then 16 ms | | 3,458 / 136.7 t/s |

What breaks:

- nvidia-smi and NVML report 250-264 MiB for Octen under unified memory, against 5,888 MiB plain. The managed pages count in the card's free figure but in no process. Every InferMux figure that adds up per-process memory would miss about 5.6 GB, among them `our_vram_mb`, which `infermux-hold` waits on, and the holders listed in the fit check's log line.
- The card's free figure no longer says what a new allocation can get. In step 4 the card showed 777 MB free and the stand-in got 3,840 MB. InferMux's fit check reads that figure (0028), so beside a managed model it would refuse a start the driver serves by eviction. Inferred from the eviction above, not tried with InferMux.
- llama.cpp under this variable also reports host memory as free, which changes what `--fit` decides. Read from the llama.cpp source, not measured.
- The stand-in's fill rate dropped to 62-181 GB/s while Octen ran queries against it, and returned to 466-575 GB/s once Octen was idle. Clocks alone explain a drop to about 174 GB/s (T7b), so only the lowest readings, 62-159 GB/s, show the two sharing the card.

Unified memory turns "the game cannot get memory" into "the model gets 40-55 times slower", and does nothing for the budget a game reads at start. For the swap problem it lets a pair that does not fit share the card by moving pages at 3.5-7.3 GB/s, which is the thrash this research was asked to end.

### Scheduling

The deep search eval waited 2 h because each client's request evicted the other's model. InferMux already has failover by capacity (0019), the fit check (0028), and groups that stay loaded together (0029, unpublished pending the user's choice on a pair that does not fit). What it lacks is a way for one client to hold the card for a while, a lease or reservation. That is a design of its own and is not researched here.

## Recommendation

The agent's, for the user to accept or not.

1. **Close the mid-game gap: A2 and A3.** Give `qwen3-asr-1.7b` and `kb-whisper-large` a `strix/cpu` place that InferMux uses while the GPU is yielded to a game, and owe another unload for any model loaded during a yield. A2 keeps dictation working, and Qwen3-ASR answers in under 2 s for 10 s of speech on the CPU. A3 is the backstop for every other client. Both respect the user's choice in 0017 not to refuse interactive requests: nothing is refused, the work moves. A1 is the simpler mechanism, but the user rejected it once.
2. **Find out what presses dikt's key in Warframe** (decision 4). If it is a game binding, the warm-ups stop at the source, and A4 is not needed.
3. **Wrap every game through gamescope.** The wrapper covers the two games that start without the hold today, and every game installed later.
4. **Stop the wallpaper in the hold, nothing else.** 330 MiB for a 1 s restore. ComfyUI's 216 MiB costs an 18 s restart and a ComfyUI job that may be queued; the rest costs apps the user uses during a game. This gives Warframe about 6% more budget. It is the smallest of the four.
5. **Do not use unified memory.** It fixes neither the game's budget nor the swap thrash, and it hides model memory from every per-process figure InferMux reads.
6. **For the model swap problem, test an Octen Q6_K** against Q8_0 on hister's queries. At about 4.9 GB it fits beside a 4.5 GB desktop, where Q8_0 failed every start through the night (nixcfg's Octen comment). It needs a quality check the agent cannot make up.

## Verification

- The in-game holder tables are read from InferMux's log lines, which list only the ten largest holders.
