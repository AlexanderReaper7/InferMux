# 0026. A deploy waits for the requests running, up to 40 s

- Date: 2026-10-14
- Status: accepted; running on both hosts since 2026-10-14 (Verification, below), except the drain while off (item 5) and review 11's two fixes (items 4 and 9), which are built and not yet deployed
- Amends [0023](0023-a-batch-request-is-a-job-paused-mid-inference.md), item 4: a job is paused and written after the drain, not at the signal. Amends [0025](0025-a-safety-stop-takes-the-local-models-off.md): the zbox's `TimeoutStopSec=5` is gone, and its controller kills the models itself when it stops the daemon. Keeps 0025's safety stops cancelling at once.

## Context

Until 2026-10-14 a stop of `infermux.service` cut every request in flight. systemd's SIGTERM went to the whole cgroup (`KillMode=control-group`), so each llama-server died with the daemon's signal, and llama-swap's own shutdown, which waits up to 30 s for the HTTP server, had nothing left to wait for. On the zbox `TimeoutStopSec=5` cut what was left after 5 s. A job was paused and written to `jobs.json` at the signal (0023, 4). Both hosts are deployed by day, by a `nix run .#switch` on strix and a `nixos-rebuild switch` to the zbox, and each restarts the daemon when its package or unit changes.

## Decision

The question put to the user, 2026-10-14: on a deploy (a stop or restart of `infermux.service` on either host), the daemon waits for running requests to finish, up to about 40 s, before it exits. The user's answer, about 12:55: "yes, make deploys wait." The coordinating session added the rest of the brief: stop taking new requests, then do what happened before at the deadline; the answer to a new request is the agent's pick, preferring one that failover or a client's retry handles; `TimeoutStopSec` above the drain on both hosts; the zbox's off file and thermal path keep cancelling at once, because they are safety stops and not deploys.

The agent built it as follows.

1. **On SIGTERM the daemon refuses every new request, waits up to 40 s for the ones running, then stops as before.** At the deadline a job's attempt is paused and written (0023, 4), an idle WebSocket session gets 1012 (0018, 7), and every other request is cut. The wait ends as soon as nothing runs, so a deploy with nothing in flight stops as fast as before.
2. **A new request gets 503 with `"type":"draining"` and `Retry-After: 5`, not a refused connection.** Failover moves a 503 to the model's next place (0016), so a request from strix to a draining zbox goes on at once. A client gets an answer that says why and when to come back; a closed listener gives it a TCP error, and Go's transport does not retry a POST on a closed keep-alive connection. 5 s is about how long a restart takes. The refusal is at the outermost handler (`internal/drain`), so a draining host forwards no new work to another host or a cloud that it would then cut.
3. **The drain waits for anything but a read.** A GET, HEAD or OPTIONS is refused like any new request but not waited for: the ones that stay open are a WebSocket session or a stream that follows something else (a job's events, llama-swap's events), and those end when what they follow does.
4. **A job's attempt is waited for like a request, and no job starts a new attempt during the drain.** The attempts run inside the daemon, so the drain counts them itself. A job waiting to retry waits until the stop, since a new attempt would only be cut. The check that the drain has begun and the count of the attempt are one step under one lock (`begin` in `internal/jobs`), which `Drain` takes too: sol's review 11 found a runner let go just before `Drain` that started its attempt after the stop had counted none.
5. **The stop drains while the local models are off too.** The user's decision, 2026-10-14 about 16:00, through the coordinating session. The question as it was put: a deploy at night on the zbox while off skipped the drain and cut the clouds' requests, as every deploy did before; keep that (A), drain while off too (B), or have the controller mark its own stop so that only that stop cuts (C). The agent recommended B. The user's answer: "B, drain while off too."
   The agent's reasoning: the stop skipped the drain so that the controller's fallback cut at once, and since item 9 the fallback no longer needs the daemon to cut. The controller kills the models itself, the start gate refuses every local start while off (0025, 7), and the warden refused and cancelled every local request when the file appeared. What a drain while off waits for is the clouds' requests, the ones forwarded to another host and the clouds' job attempts, none of them on this host's card. The cost: in the fallback, a daemon that left a model running stays up for up to 85 s after its SIGTERM. It takes no new request, its models are already killed, and one it starts anyway the controller's watch kills again, since the watch runs for as long as the pause.
6. **`KillMode=mixed`, so the SIGTERM reaches only the daemon.** Under `control-group` the llama-servers died with the signal and the drain would have nothing to drain. After the daemon exits, systemd sends SIGKILL to whatever it left.
7. **`TimeoutStopSec = 90` on both hosts, set in `nix/module.nix`.** The longest stop is the drain's 40 s, the jobs' 10 s, and llama-swap's own shutdown, which its backstop forces at 35 s: 85 s. The zbox's own 5 s is gone.
8. **The drain runs before llama-swap's backstop starts its clock.** The hook in `llama-swap.go` moved from after the backstop goroutine to before it; otherwise the backstop's `os.Exit(1)` at 35 s ends a 40 s drain.
9. **The zbox controller's fallback kills the models itself and does not wait.** Under `KillMode=mixed` its `systemctl stop` would reach only the daemon, and the daemon is what left a model running while off. So it sends `systemctl stop --no-block` and then SIGKILL to every process in the unit's cgroup but the unit's `MainPID`, the daemon, and its `ControlPID`, `prepare`. A blocking stop could now hold the controller's loop for 90 s. Each process listed in `cgroup.procs` is held by a descriptor of its `/proc` directory before the two pids are read, its cgroup is read through that descriptor, and the SIGKILL goes through it with `pidfd_send_signal`. Sol's review 11 found the two reads the other way round, so a daemon that restarted between them was in the list and was killed, and a pid reused before the kill went to whoever had it. The check is the cgroup and not the program's name, because immich-ml's gunicorn and workers are models too.
10. **The fallback sends SIGKILL, with no SIGTERM first.** The user's decision, 2026-10-14 about 16:00, through the coordinating session. The question as it was put: the fallback SIGKILLs the models where systemd used to send SIGTERM and wait 5 s; keep SIGKILL, or have the controller send SIGTERM and SIGKILL 5 s later. The agent recommended keeping it. The user's answer: "keep SIGKILL."
    The agent's reason, given with the recommendation: the zbox's models, Octen's llama-server and immich-ml, keep nothing on disk that a kill loses. Octen has no `--slot-save-path`, and immich-ml writes only its model cache, while it downloads one. The driver frees a process's VRAM when it exits, however it exits; that was not measured under SIGKILL. llama-swap already SIGKILLs a model that ignores its SIGTERM (`process_command.go`). The fallback fires only after a model ran 10 s while off, when every local request was already refused and cancelled, and a job lives in the daemon, which the controller does not kill. What SIGTERM bought was an exit code and the last log lines. Neither `TimeoutStopSec` nor `ExecStop` gives a grace period instead: under `mixed` the timeout bounds only the wait for the daemon and the kill goes around systemd, and an `ExecStop` runs on every stop, so it would stop the models during a deploy's drain.

## Consequences

- A deploy takes up to 40 s longer while something runs, about 85 s at worst before systemd's SIGKILL. `nix run .#switch` has no timeout of its own and waits.
- strix's switch stops its system units before it restarts the user units, so a switch that restarts both InferMux and T3 restarts T3 only after InferMux's drain. That includes the T3 V2 cutover's switch, should it restart InferMux too.
- During the drain the host's own clients get 503s: Codex, Episteme, the UI. How long Codex keeps retrying a 503 was not checked; a turn that starts during a drain fails once its retries run out. A request from another host fails over (0016).
- A WebSocket session is closed with 1012 after the drain rather than waited for.
- A job running past 40 s is paused and written as before, and resumes when the daemon is back (0023).

## Verification

Tests, run 2026-10-14:

- `internal/drain`: a running request finishes and a new one gets 503 `draining` with Retry-After; the cut ends what is left and a GET is not waited for; the wait returns at zero or at the deadline.
- `internal/jobs`: `TestADrainLetsTheRunningAttemptFinishAndStartsNoOther`, and `TestADrainBetweenWaitAndAttemptStartsNothing`, sol's review 11 reproduction: the runner let go, then `Drain`, then the attempt.
- `infermux_drain_test.go`, through the whole daemon: a deploy lets the request running finish and refuses a new one; a job still running at the deadline is paused and written, and a request still running then is cut; a job waiting to retry starts no attempt during the drain; a stop while off waits for a cloud's request, which finishes whole.
- nixcfg's controller tests, with real processes in a cgroup file the test writes: the kill spares the daemon and `prepare`; a daemon that restarts while the controller reads is not killed, sol's review 11 reproduction; a listed pid whose process is outside the unit's cgroup is not killed.
- Each of these mutations failed its test: no `Start`, the job attempts not waited for, no wait, the off file's skip put back, no cut, the jobs not drained, the handler not wrapped, the drain's check apart from the attempt's count. In nixcfg's controller tests: no kill, a blocking stop, the daemon killed with the models, `MainPID` read before `cgroup.procs`, no cgroup check, `prepare` not spared.
- Not covered by a test: a pid reused between the controller opening its `/proc` directory and the signal. No test can make the kernel reuse a pid on cue; that the signal through the descriptor reaches the process opened or none is `pidfd_send_signal`'s contract.
- Not covered by a test: the hook's position above the backstop in `llama-swap.go`, which only a live stop longer than 35 s shows, and `KillMode`.

Watched 2026-10-14, after nixcfg `550d1c6` on both hosts (details in `docs/verification.md`):

- A cloud chat streaming through a restart on the zbox finished with `[DONE]` 34.2 s into the drain, and the restart returned 0.4 s later.
- An Octen batch on the zbox's GPU answered 200 28.3 s after the signal: under `KillMode=mixed` its llama-server lived through the drain.
- A job still running at 40 s was paused and written, the stop took 41.6 s, past the backstop's 35 s, and the job finished as attempt 2 after the restart.
- On strix a cloud chat still writing at 40 s was cut at 40.02 s; another client's chat on the GPU, in flight at the signal, answered 200 inside the drain.
- A new chat and a GET during a drain each got 503 `draining` with `Retry-After: 5`, on both hosts.

Not watched: a stop while off, the controller's fallback killing the models through their descriptors, a WebSocket closed with 1012, a request failing over from one host's 503 `draining` to the other, and Codex's handling of that 503.
